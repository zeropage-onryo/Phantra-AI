You write the script of a short MOTION-GRAPHICS ad: no footage, no actors -- big
type, one huge faint word behind each line, screens of the product on cards, a small
label now and then, over flat brand colours, with a voiceover and a music bed under
it. Think of a polished product launch video: one idea per beat, a few words per
idea, a clear arc from the problem to the product to the call to action.

THE SHAPE that works (follow it unless the brief asks for something else):
1. THE PROBLEM, first: one or two beats on what is wrong today, said bluntly
   ("Managing talent shouldn't feel like this."), on the primary colour.
2. THE ANSWER: the product's name or its promise, landing hard.
3. ONE FEATURE PER BEAT: a short headline, its big word behind it, and the screen
   that proves it on a card beside it. Most of the ad is these beats.
4. TWO SIDES, when the brand serves two audiences (businesses and workers, buyers
   and sellers): the features for one side, then the other, each side in its own
   run of beats, and a line that names both near the end.
5. THE PAYOFF: a short beat or two on the primary colour that sum it up in three
   words or fewer each ("Your skills." then "Your schedule." then "Your rate.").
6. THE CALL TO ACTION, last.
A screen always rides on a CARD beside its headline; never put a screen of the app
full frame.

THE LENGTH: about {seconds} seconds in total, in {beats_min} to {beats_max} beats.
Most beats are 2-4 seconds; the opening and the call to action may be a little
longer. The beat seconds should add up to roughly {seconds}.

THE PRODUCT'S SCREENS, by number (a beat may show one of them on a card beside its
headline; never invent a screen that is not listed):
{cards}

THE BRAND'S PHOTOS, by number -- real photographs, not screens (a beat may sit on one
of them FULL FRAME instead of a flat colour; use them sparingly, for a physical product
or a real place; never invent a photo that is not listed):
{pictures}

FOR EVERY BEAT write:
- "headline": the line on screen. Short -- at most 8 words, usually 3-6. Sentence
  case, ending with a full stop. Every headline different. No hashtags, no emoji.
- "big_word": ONE word, 3-10 letters, that sums the beat up (FIND, BOOK, RATE,
  SKILLS). It is set huge and faint behind the headline. "" for a beat that
  should not have one (the opening and the call to action usually do not).
- "card": the number of the screenshot this beat shows, or null. Use each
  screenshot where it fits the line best; a screenshot may appear more than once.
- "card_caption": a few words under the card ("Alex R. · Line cook"), or "".
- "label": a small pill of text -- a price, a time, a name or a place the brief or
  the brand gives, or the website -- or "". At most 4 words. Use it on a beat or
  two, not all.
- "background": which brand colour the beat sits on: "background" for most beats,
  "primary" for one or two beats that need to land hard (the product's name, the
  call to action).
- "seconds": how long the beat lasts.
- "picture": the number of the brand photo this beat sits on, full frame, or null
  for a flat colour. Use photos where showing the real product or place sells the
  line; keep the flat colours for the bold type beats and the call to action.
- "move": the camera move on that photo, one of: {moves}. "hold" for a still
  frame; "scale_in" settles the picture into place (good for a product reveal).
  Always "hold" when "picture" is null.
- "voiceover": the words spoken over this beat, or "". Short enough to say in the
  beat's seconds (about 2.5 words a second). Read in order, the lines are ONE
  script, so they flow from beat to beat; they do not just repeat the headline.

Rules:
- FACTS ONLY FROM ABOVE. A price, a rating, a number, a statistic, a time, a
  customer's or a worker's name may appear in a headline, a label or a card
  caption ONLY when the brief or the brand above gives it. Never invent one: an
  ad with a made-up price or rating is a false claim. When there is no fact to
  put on a label, leave the label "".
- Follow the brand's tone, its words to use and avoid, and its claims rules
  exactly. Make no claim about a product the brand does not allow. The same
  rules hold for every voiceover line.
- Never describe what an app screen or a photo shows beyond what its name above
  says: do not invent buttons, features, dates or numbers on a screen.
- The last beat is the call to action, in the brand's call-to-action style when
  it has one.
- Write only the JSON object the schema asks for: a short "title" for the ad, and
  the "beats" in order.
