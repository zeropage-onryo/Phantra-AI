# UGC-style clip

A clip that looks like a real customer filmed it on their phone: handheld, vertical, natural light, a real room. It works because it does NOT look like an ad. Every choice below pushes away from gloss.

## Settle it

Ask at most two things, as choices, and skip what is already known:

- The format: a review to camera, an unboxing, a "get ready with me", a before-and-after, a day in the life.
- Who is on camera. A person on camera must be one of their character elements with photos, or attached to this turn. Faces are never invented or found on the web: without a photo, ask for one, or offer a hands-only version where no face is shown.

## The look, every time

- 9:16, handheld at arm's length or propped on a shelf, small natural wobble.
- Light from a real window or a ceiling lamp; slightly uneven, never a studio key light.
- A lived-in room: a kitchen counter, a bathroom shelf, a car seat. A little clutter.
- Phone-camera texture: a touch of noise, ordinary colour, no film grade, no shallow cinematic depth of field.

## Shape it

Use the multi-shot recipe, with two to four short shots. Start mid-sentence or mid-gesture -- real clips never begin with a setup. If the person speaks, write the line in quotes, short and casual, in their words, and keep it under about 12 words a shot. The product, when there is one, is the EXACT object from its photos, held and used the way a person really would.

Add to the avoid list: studio lighting, perfect skin, a glossy ad look, slow motion, cinematic camera moves, text on screen, a voice that sounds like a narrator.

## Hand it over

- Keep `brief` as the full prompt.
- When they ask for it to be made: make_video with the prompt as the idea. Suggest a model that is good with people and speech when they have not picked one (list_models says which); never decide it for them.

## Check before you send

Vertical? Handheld? Real light and a real room? Does it start mid-action? Is everyone on camera from a photo, and every line short and in their voice?
