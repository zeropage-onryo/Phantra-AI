# Ad

A short film that sells one thing. Most ads here are 6 to 15 seconds and are watched with the sound off, on a phone, by someone about to scroll past. So the first second has to stop them, the product has to be unmistakable, and the last frame has to say what to do.

## Settle the brief first

Ask at most two things, as choices, and skip what the conversation or the project's brief already answers:

- The ONE thing the viewer should remember (the product's one benefit, not a list).
- Where it runs and how long: a 9:16 social ad, a 16:9 pre-roll, a 1:1 feed post; 6, 10 or 15 seconds.

The product must be one of their elements with photos (a product element) or attached to this turn. Without a photograph the product will be invented and will not match the real one -- ask for it before writing.

## Shape it in three beats

1. Hook (the first 1-2 seconds). Start mid-action on something that makes a person look: the product in motion, a surprising scale, a problem happening. Never a logo, a slow fade-in or a wide establishing shot.
2. Proof (the middle). The product doing its one job, close enough to read its shape, colour and label. One clear action per shot. The product is named and locked as the EXACT object from its reference photos in every shot it appears in.
3. Payoff (the last 2 seconds). The result of using it, then a clean, still frame of the product that holds long enough to read. Leave space in that frame for the person's own text or logo -- do not write text into the picture; models garble letters.

## Write it

Use the multi-shot recipe for the prompt itself: a look line, timed shots, a sound line and an avoid list. Add to the avoid list: garbled text, fake labels, extra logos, the product changing shape or colour between shots. Sound: one sound that sells the action (a cap clicking, a pour, a zip), and no music unless they asked for it.

## Hand it over

- Keep `brief` as the full prompt, self-contained.
- When they ask for it to be made: make_video with the prompt as the idea, and seconds set to the length they chose.
- Offer one variation in a single sentence (a different hook, or the same ad in another shape) -- do not make it unless they say yes.

## Check before you send

Does the first second stop a scroll? Is there ONE benefit? Is the product named as EXACT from its photos in every shot it is in? Is the last frame still and clear, with room for their text? No invented text or logos in the picture?
