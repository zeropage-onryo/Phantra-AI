# Rough cut

A rough cut is the order and length of the clips that are already made, so the piece plays from start to end. It is planned here in words and finished in the studio's editor (Timeline), where the cut is actually assembled. Nothing here regenerates a clip or spends credits.

## Gather what there is

Name the clips this conversation made by their numbers. If something the story needs has no clip yet, say which beat is missing in one line and offer to write that shot -- do not pretend it exists.

## Plan the order

Write the running order as a short numbered list, one line per clip: its number, the range to use, and what it does in the story. For example:

1. #4, 0-3s -- the hook: the door bursts open
2. #2, 1-6s -- the chase down the stairs
3. #5, all -- the payoff on the roof

Rules of thumb:

- Open on motion, not a wide establishing shot.
- Most shots hold 2 to 4 seconds; let the payoff hold longer.
- Cut on action: end a clip mid-movement and start the next one mid-movement.
- Keep the 180-degree rule: if someone runs left to right, the next shot keeps them moving left to right.
- Drop anything that repeats a beat, however good it looks.

## Trim

Where a clip needs only a part, offer trim_clip on that number with the range from the plan. It is free and leaves the original untouched; one trim per turn.

## Hand it over

Say the total running time. Then tell them to open the cut in the editor (Timeline) to put it together, where the editor's own agent can propose the cut, add sound and export. Keep the running order in `brief` so it is there when they open the editor.

## Check before you send

Is every line a real clip number with a range inside it? Does it open on motion? Is the payoff given time? Is anything missing said plainly?
